01
The Need

The need has never been greater.

The world is louder, faster, and more confused than at any point in living memory. Families are searching for a refuge from the noise. Saints across continents are asking the same quiet question: outside of official Church-produced media, is there anywhere I can find media I can trust to feed my soul, work made with real craft, not generated to fill a feed, that increases the light without offending it?

Mainstream platforms bury faith-aligned work between content that contradicts everything Saints stand for. The "clean" alternatives feel dated, niche, and apologetic. Parents reach for the remote a dozen times an evening. Children grow up quoting lines no one wanted them to learn.

This is a hunger. And it has been growing for a generation.

02
The Opportunity

And the opportunity has never been greater.

Three things have changed that make now the perfect time to do things differently. They are arriving together, in the same few years that the need has peaked. That is not coincidence. It is a window.

The Saints are connected. A creator in Idaho can reach a family in the Philippines tonight. Distance is no longer a gatekeeper. The body of Christ has never been this reachable to itself.

The tools are in your hands. AI, modern production software, and accessible publishing: dreams that used to require a studio and a budget now require a calling and the will to begin. The gap between imagining a faith-filled film, album, story, or series and making it has never been smaller.

The audience is waiting. They are actively searching for what you can make. They will pay for it. They will share it. They will raise their children on it.

The barriers that kept faith-aligned creators on the sidelines for fifty years (distribution, production cost, finding a like-minded audience) are collapsing. Now is the time for creators to create faith-building and faith-sustaining content to meet the needs of the broader family of Saints worldwide.

03
The Stakes

What we consume shapes who we become.

This is not a slogan. It is doctrine, and it is observation, and every parent who has watched a child quote a song lyric they didn't know was being learned has felt it.

What plays in our cars, on our phones, in our living rooms, and in our headphones is shaping the rising generation moment by moment. The stakes are not just commercial. They are not even just cultural. They are eternal.

So much media asks to become a replacement for God. It entices us to surrender by increment: our time, our attention, our affections, our devotion.

The work that belongs on SaintStream does the opposite. It doesn't compete with God for a person's heart. It points them back to Him. A song that turns a listener toward repentance. A story that makes a child want to be good. A film that strengthens the desire to pray. That is the standard. Not merely clean, but consecrated.

Because of you:

Every one of those outcomes began with a creator who didn't yet feel ready. You don't have to be ready either. You only have to begin.

SaintStream is being built as a nonprofit (formation in process). It has no shareholders and no profit to extract. Of every dollar it receives, currently about 70% flows to creators and the rest sustains the organization: hosting, the review process, payment processing, and the people and services that keep SaintStream running. Any surplus goes back into those people and services, never into anyone's pocket.

At its heart, SaintStream is a streaming service. One subscription funds one shared pool, and you earn from that pool whenever subscribers play, watch, hear, or read your work. Buying a title to own is an optional bucket on top that pays for itself. Open any question below. Nothing here is hidden.

How does streaming create income for me?

Every paid subscription feeds one shared creator pool. You earn a set rate for each real unit of your work that subscribers consume: a play, a minute listened or watched, a percent of a book read. That rate is the same no matter what other creators or lanes are doing.

Free, lean-back listening is for discovery, and it pays little. The income arrives the moment a subscriber commits and engages your work, month after month, for as long as they keep coming back to it.

The pool is a ceiling, not a divisor: as long as total payouts stay under it, everyone earns the full rate. If you'd like to also sell a title for people to own, that is a separate bucket that pays for itself.

How music pays

Your songs stream free on radio for discovery. When a subscriber plays your song on demand, each play earns at your per-play rate from the shared pool.

You can also offer a track as a buy-to-own download for listeners who want it permanently, an optional bucket on top of streaming that pays for itself.

Target rate, when the pool is full: about $0.02 per subscriber play. Run your own numbers in the calculator below.

How films, podcasts, audiobooks & ebooks pay

All of these stream under the subscription, included for every subscriber. You earn by what is actually consumed: minutes watched for film, minutes heard for podcasts and audiobooks, and percent read for ebooks. A feature film and a five-minute meditation are each paid fairly because both are measured by real time consumed.

If you'd like, any title can also be offered for purchase so a listener can own and download it, a separate bucket on top that pays for itself.

Target rates, when the pool is full: about $0.005 per minute, and about $0.003 per percent of a book read. Run your own numbers in the calculator below.

Where does the 30% go?

SaintStream is a nonprofit, so the 30% is not profit, it's the actual cost of keeping the promise: hosting and streaming, the human + AI review that keeps the catalog safe, payment processing, support, and building the platform.

No advertising means no ad revenue, subscriptions and purchases are the whole engine. Keeping that 30% lean is how the mission stays funded without a single ad.

Creating as a team or a band?

Royalties are paid to a single account. A band, a studio, or a collaboration designates one account to receive them.

How those earnings are divided among collaborators is your own arrangement, SaintStream pays the account; the split among members is yours to manage.

Does this work the same in every country?

The figures on this page are shown for a subscriber in the United States, in US dollars. In other countries, a subscription is priced for that local economy, not for New York, so anyone can afford to take part. Because subscription prices set the size of the pool, payouts scale with them. Both sides of the equation move together.

The goal is equity, not just access. An honest hour of your creative work is meant to bless your life the same whether you live in Idaho or India, measured by what it actually buys where you are. The same dignity, and the same reward for the same work, so Saints in every nation can enjoy the same blessings and contribute their own gifts.

A bigger slice of the right pie.

The Latter-day Saint audience is smaller than the whole world, and that is an advantage, not a limitation. On a mainstream platform your work is one of a hundred million tracks, paid a fraction of a cent, and nearly impossible to find. On SaintStream you are one of a curated catalog, paid from the creator pool by an audience that came here specifically for work like yours.

A large share of five thousand intentional plays, from people who share your faith and chose to be here, is worth far more, in both dollars and reach, than a sliver of a sliver on a platform where no one is looking for you. A smaller pie, but a far larger slice, and a real chance to actually be discovered.

And here's the part most platforms hide: the more subscribers join, the larger the pool grows, for everyone. Ship work that brings the Saints to SaintStream this season, and you're not just publishing, you're growing the very pool that pays you.

A call for content the Saints can't find anywhere else.

SaintStream isn't another shelf for media that already exists. It's a place for work that wouldn't exist without the creators it was built for.

And, Honestly

Not every project will be a great success.

Not every song you record will be sung in seventeen languages. Not every children's cartoon will be quoted at family dinner tables for a generation. Not every proposed project will reach its funding goal. Not every story you pour months into will find the audience you imagined for it.

That is true of every creative life. It has been true since the first hymn was written and the first parable was told. It is not a sign that you shouldn't have begun.

SaintStream is a place where you can change it. Refine it. Rework it. Set it down, learn what it taught you, and put something new back out there.

You don't get one shot here. You get a home, and a Tribe, and as many beginnings as a faithful creative life requires. The Saints don't need a creator who guarantees every project will be a masterpiece. They need a creator who keeps showing up.

But there is a large body of Saints the world over that are going to be thrilled at the prospect of consuming the media that uplifts and inspires, strengthens and realigns.
